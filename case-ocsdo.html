Case study

Showing How Open Science and AI Accelerate Discovery

NASA Office of the Chief Science Data Officer

  • Briefing Presentations
  • Annual Reports
  • Conference Exhibits
  • Communication Strategy
  • AI & Open Science

Challenge

NASA’s missions produce enormous amounts of data, and making sense of it depends on supercomputers, AI, and modern data systems. The Office of the Chief Science Data Officer runs that backbone. It manages NASA’s high-end computing, keeps mission data open, accessible, and preserved, and brings AI and data science into research. That work underpins discoveries in solar science, human spaceflight, and more. But it happens behind the scenes, and the office needed scientists, leaders, and partners to see why open science and AI matter.

My Solution

I designed the briefing decks the office’s leaders and team presented at conferences, meetings, and agency events. In them I translated technical concepts like supercomputing, AI for science, and open data into clear stories about what they make possible for NASA. I ran the office’s conference booths in 2025, including at the American Astronomical Society (AAS) and American Geophysical Union (AGU) conferences, putting its work in front of the science community in person. I also produced the OCSDO Year in Review reports for 2024 and 2025, which capture each year’s progress in one public story.

Impact

A traveling team equipped with consistent, compelling presentations, a visible presence at major science conferences, and two public annual reports on science.nasa.gov.

I created 30+ briefing presentations reaching 3,000+ audience members at national and international conferences.
